Core technology accepted to ICRA 2026

Hadeon's paper, "Accelerating Trajectory Optimization by Exploiting B-Spline Gradient Structure" — co-authored by Nikos Doiron, Thomas Duquette, Gilde Vanel Tchane Djogdom and André Gallant — was accepted to ICRA 2026, the IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation, one of the field's most competitive academic venues. Doiron and Gallant presented the work at the conference.

The paper details the gradient-computation techniques behind BLAST's speed: exploiting the local support of B-splines to skip unnecessary gradient evaluations, replacing finite-difference approximations with closed-form derivatives, and aggregating constraints per knot-span to shrink the optimization problem. Tested on a simulated UR5e across 64 tasks in a cluttered workspace, the combined techniques cut computation time by up to 96.3% — a 26.9x speedup — over a finite-difference baseline, with no loss of trajectory quality, success rate, or fidelity to the kinematic, dynamic and collision constraints.

Getting the work in front of the academic community was a deliberate step — a motion-planning engine selling on the promise of provable safety should have its core math held to peer-review standard, not just marketing copy. The paper's data and code are public on BLAST's repository, the same open-source library behind the engine.
